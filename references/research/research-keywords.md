## How claims are graded

- **(a)** Google's own documentation or API reference, or a study with a stated sample of thousands of accounts or campaigns.
- **(b)** A named practitioner or vendor with a method, a date and some data, or a Google claim relayed by a reputable outlet.
- **(c)** Single-practitioner opinion, an undated blog, a re-quoted statistic whose original source could not be checked, or a snippet-only source.

Where two sources disagree, the higher grade wins and the disagreement is noted.

## Findings by area

### 1. Keyword Planner mechanics

- **Volume is an average of the last 12 months, for the keyword AND its close variants, rounded, and specific to the location, language and network you chose** · (a) sources 1, 2. Because of rounding, "when you get keyword ideas for multiple locations, the search volumes might not add up as you'd expect" (a) source 1. So a per-city pull never sums to the service-area pull. Pull the whole service area as one geo set.
- **Historical stats are exact-match stats regardless of match type entered** · (a) source 1. Volume for "plumber" is the volume for [plumber] and its close variants, not for phrase or broad reach.
- **Forecasts differ from historical metrics**: forecasts take bid, budget, seasonality and match type into account; historical metrics do not · (a) source 1. For a budget read use forecasts or do the arithmetic yourself; for "is there demand" use historical.
- **Bucketed ranges vs exact numbers**: accounts with no or low spend see ranges like 1K to 10K; accounts with an active campaign and spend see exact averages · (b) sources 49, 50. **Google has never published the threshold** · (b) source 50. Practitioner figures of "$5 to $10 a day" (snippet, (c)) and "one dollar a month" (existing file, (c)) are not documented. The Google community thread in source 70 shows accounts with live campaigns still seeing ranges at times. Treat any dollar threshold as unverified.
- **Top of page bid low and high** = the 20th and 80th percentile of what advertisers paid for top-of-page in the last 30 days, for your location and network · (a) sources 1, 2. It "assumes a search query exactly matches your keyword" and is "not a guarantee" · (a) source 10. Practitioners average the two as a CPC guess and "err toward overestimation" for new accounts because Quality Score starts low · (c) source 51. No published study on estimate vs realised CPC was found.
- **The API fields** (a) source 2: `avg_monthly_searches` (12-month average), `monthly_search_volumes` (12 monthly points, use for seasonality), `competition` (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH enum), `competition_index` (0 to 100), `low_top_of_page_bid_micros`, `high_top_of_page_bid_micros`, `average_cpc_micros` (only when `historical_metrics_options.include_average_cpc` is set). Request takes `geo_target_constants` (list, so a whole service area is one call), `language`, `keyword_plan_network` (GOOGLE_SEARCH or GOOGLE_SEARCH_AND_PARTNERS), `include_adult_keywords`. Metrics update monthly.
- **GenerateKeywordIdeas** (a) source 3: seeds are keyword (max 20 per request), URL, site, or keyword plus URL. Returns up to 700 ideas per request with `keyword_idea_metrics` of the same shape, plus `close_variants`. Multiple geos allowed per call.
- **Localized forecasting**, 7 Jul 2025: Keyword Planner forecasts can now be broken down by city, region, DMA and device · (b) source 30. This is the sanctioned way to see "which cities inside my area carry the demand" without building city keywords.
- **Competition column is for the location and network you picked**, and Semrush's paid equivalent is Competitive Density, 0 to 1 · (a) source 1, (b) source 46.

### 2. Match types in 2025-2026

- **Exact is not exact.** Exact match triggers on misspellings, plurals, stemming, abbreviations, accents, reordered words, added or removed function words, implied words, synonyms and paraphrases, and "same intent" searches · (a) source 13. Close variants apply to every match type with no opt-out · (a) source 13.
- **Phrase = "includes the meaning of your keyword"**, broad = "related to your keyword, which can include searches that don't contain the direct meaning" · (a) source 12. Broad matching also uses the user's recent searches, landing page content and other keywords in the ad group · (a) source 12.
- **Matching priority**: an exact keyword identical to the query wins; then phrase and broad identical to the query; then AI relevance; then Ad Rank · (a) source 12. Practitioners observed in 2025 that near-variants increasingly route to broad keywords or PMax rather than the exact keyword · (c) source 45, (b) source 32.
- **Broad is Google's default match type and Google says Smart Bidding is critical with it** · (a) source 11. Google's own claim: moving phrase to broad in tCPA campaigns gives about 25% more conversions, about 12% more value in tROAS · (a) as a Google claim, source 4. Independent lift figures are lower: a "10% uplift" claim relayed in source 27 (b).
- **What the large studies show for lead gen**:
  - Adalysis, Sep 2025, 16,825 campaigns: exact is 54% of keywords, 23% of impressions, 40% of conversions, with the best CTR, conversion rate and CPA. Under Maximize Conversions (lead gen) exact wins; phrase has the most conversions (41%) but the worst CPA; broad has the lowest CPA but the lowest conversion rate. Under Target CPA (lead gen) exact wins, phrase "edges out" broad on CTR and conversion rate, broad takes 51% of impressions. Recommended path: exact, then phrase, then broad · (a) source 18.
  - Optmyzr, Mar 2024, 4,000+ accounts: exact beat broad on conversion rate in 62% of accounts, on CPA in 74%, on CTR in 82%, and "the gap closed quite a bit" versus prior years · (a) source 19.
  - Optmyzr, Nov 2024, 992,028 keywords: exact ROAS 415%, phrase 313%, broad 277%; broad share of keywords rose from 33% to 37% between 2022 and 2024 · (a) source 20.
  - Logical Position, Jul 2025, 7,000+ accounts: phrase CPC rose 43% Jun 2023 to Jun 2025, broad CPC rose 29% · (a) for the data, source 28.
- **Reconciled reading**: exact has the best unit economics, phrase is the widest safe net for a new lead-gen account without conversion data, broad is a volume lever once Smart Bidding has signal. Phrase as the default for a new local account is supported by sources 18, 29, 43 (b to a). Bigeye's "start with 20 to 30% broad" is (c) and contradicts source 18 for lead gen.
- **Broad match preconditions from practitioners**: Smart Bidding on a conversion goal, 30+ conversions a month at campaign level (Google's baseline in source 14 is "at least 30 conversions, 50 for Target ROAS" (a)), a working negative infrastructure, brand controls, and ideally offline or qualified-lead conversions imported · (b) sources 27, 28, 34, 41. Avoid broad when budget is thin, CPCs are high, or nobody will read the search terms report weekly · (b) source 34.
- **Brand inclusions and exclusions** exist for broad-only campaigns and for AI Max · (a) sources 8, 27.
- **Broad match campaign setting**: Google was reported in Dec 2024 to auto-toggle broad on when advertisers moved to conversion bidding; the Ads Liaison called it "not expected" and under investigation · (b) source 33. Audit match types after any bid strategy change.
- **Phrase "absorbed" modified broad** (retired Jul 2021) and now includes reordering and synonyms · (b) source 43.

### 3. The no-city rule - evidence for and against

For the rule (keywords are bare service terms, location is a campaign setting):

- Location targeting has two options. "Presence or interest" is the default and reaches "people who have shown interest in your targeted locations"; "Presence" reaches people "likely to be located, or regularly located" there · (a) source 5. Interest is inferred from "terms used in their searches" among other signals · (a) source 5. So a city-modified keyword plus the default setting shows ads to people anywhere who typed the city.
- Interest-only targeting was removed in 2023; the only remaining split is Presence vs Presence-or-interest · (b) source 56.
- Google's own +5% conversion claim for Presence-or-interest is specific to Travel, Real Estate and Education · (c) source 57 relaying Google. No source claims it for home services.
- Keyword Planner volume is per location setting and per close-variant cluster (a) source 1, so the bare term pulled with the service area as geo already includes searchers in that area. The city-modified term is a subset by construction. Per-city volumes are rounded and do not sum (a) source 1.
- Exact and phrase keywords match on meaning, and "toronto plumber" does not carry the same meaning as "plumber" (a) sources 12, 13. So a Toronto searcher typing "plumber" or "plumber near me" cannot be matched by an exact or phrase [toronto plumber]. Only a broad "toronto plumber" could, and then only because broad ignores the city word, which defeats the purpose.
- Adalysis found "near me" queries being routed to a pricing ad group under AI Max despite identical keywords in a location ad group · (b) source 32. Geo-modified ad groups do not give you routing control anyway.
- Local intent is mostly implicit. Google's figure that 46% of searches carry local intent and the reported 900% two-year growth in "near me" and "close by" queries are re-quoted through source 62 (c); the direction is not in dispute, the exact numbers are.
- Smart Bidding already uses "physical location and location intent" as a bid signal · (a) source 14. Bid adjustments by city inside one campaign are the supported way to pay more for a hot suburb.

Against the rule, or nuance:

- Practitioners still recommend geo-modified keywords "alongside" location targeting, on the argument that explicit city typers are higher intent · (c) source 58. No data offered.
- Reverse location targeting is a legitimate use of city keywords: a separate campaign targeting people OUTSIDE the area who type the city, for businesses that serve travellers or relocators · (b) source 36. Not a local trade case.
- City-modified terms do exist in volume for big cities (the retired example file shows "plumber toronto" at 8,100 a month). The correct reading is that these searches are already inside the bare-term volume pulled with the geo filter, and are already matched by phrase "plumber" (phrase allows words before and after) with Presence targeting. They are not lost by skipping the keyword. This is inference from (a) sources 1, 12, 13.
- One real cost of the rule: with the city out of the keyword, the city must be in the ad and on the page for relevance. Location insertion in responsive search ads does this at city, state or country level with a fallback text · (b) from prior knowledge; the help page could not be fetched today (404 on the URL tried), so grade (b) not (a).

Verdict: the rule holds for local service accounts. Evidence is (a) on mechanism and (c) against. The only carve-out is a deliberately separate out-of-area campaign (source 36), which is not a local trade case.

### 4. Buyer-intent modifiers for services and what converts

- Home services conversion rates by subcategory, 2025 (a) source 21: cleaning 17.65%, handyman 13.45%, painting 10.80%, electrical 9.08%, plumbing 7.63%, heating 7.48%, AC 6.56%, roofing 3.70%, general contractors 2.61%. Overall 7.33%. CPL: cleaning $46.99, handyman $54.05, electrical $93.69, plumbing $129.02, HVAC around $128, roofing $228.15. CPC: painting $13.74, electrical $12.18, roofing $10.70, plumbing $10.49, HVAC $9.30 to $9.68, general contractors $5.31. Overall CPC $7.85.
- Cross-industry 2025 (a) source 22: CPC $5.42 (up 16.3% year on year), conversion rate 8.18%, CPL $70.11. Legal CPC $8.58 with a 2.55% conversion rate; automotive repair converts best at 14.67%. "Accounts using negative keywords saw conversion rates up to three times higher"; about 29% of accounts recorded zero conversions in 90 days.
- 2026 relay (b) source 23: home improvement CPC $8.33, conversion 8.05%, CPL $90.92; legal CPC $9.87, conversion 5.55%, CPL $131.63.
- LSA cost per lead, 100+ clients, Aug 2025 (b) source 63: plumbing $69, HVAC $80, roofing $162, personal injury $249, locksmith $34, landscaper $39. Useful as a floor: if Search CPL is far above LSA CPL, LSA is the cheaper lead.
- No large study was found ranking modifiers ("emergency" vs "repair" vs "cost" vs "near me") by conversion rate. The "8 to 20% for emergency and near me, lower for cost and best" range in a snippet is (c). The structural argument stands: emergency and symptom searchers are mid-crisis and price-insensitive; "cost", "how much" and "best" searchers are comparing. Treat modifier-level conversion as a test variable per account, not a production rule.
- Symptom terms: Google Search itself classifies these by what ranks; if the top results are tutorials the query is DIY-leaning · (b) existing file, consistent with source 29's intent buckets.
- "Free" and "quote" and "cost" are two-sided: "free estimate" and "get a quote" are buyers · (b) sources 26, 27 warn about over-blocking; source 27 lists "free" among safe account-level negatives for B2B but not for trades. Keep the existing warning.

### 5. Keywords per ad group and structure

- SKAGs are dead because close variants make one-keyword isolation impossible and it starves Smart Bidding · (b) sources 38, 43. Google's matching hierarchy (a) source 12 makes routing by match type unreliable.
- Counts: "avoid 15+ in one group" (b) source 29; "10 to 15 good ones beat 50" (c) source 47; "under 30" (c) source 48; "5 to 10 intent-tight" (c) source 44 and the 2026 snippets. Convention: **5 to 15 close synonyms sharing one ad and one page**. The existing file's "3 to 20" and "10 to 20" are at the wide end; tighten to 5 to 15.
- Match type segmentation across campaigns or ad groups is dead; group all match types of a theme together · (b) source 38.
- Pair broad with exact in the same account and add proven queries as exact; use ad-group exact negatives so a broad keyword does not cannibalise a proven exact · (b) sources 41, 19.
- Quality Score: three components, expected CTR, ad relevance, landing page experience · (a) via sources 59, 61 restating Google. The 39/39/22 weighting seen in a snippet could not be sourced and is (c); Google publishes no weights. A Feb 2025 predictive landing-page model can rate a page "below average" before it has traffic, and judges navigational clarity, transparency and unexpected destination · (c) source 60, single vendor. Keyword-to-ad-to-page alignment remains "the single biggest lever" for relevance · (c) source 59, consistent with Google guidance.

### 6. Seed expansion and free sources

- SEL's process (b) source 29: 1 to 3 word seeds from the service list, then Keyword Planner, AnswerThePublic for questions, autocomplete for trending phrasing, Semrush for competitors, an LLM for seed expansion; classify into brand, competitor, third-party brand, generic category, generic specific, top-of-funnel. Avoid terms under 50 searches a month unless niche.
- Keyword Planner URL and site seeds pull ideas from a competitor's page or domain · (a) source 3. That is a free competitor keyword source that does not need Semrush.
- Autocomplete reflects real searches; People Also Ask gives 4 to 5 questions per query · (c) tool-vendor pages. Fine for phrasing and symptom discovery, useless for volume.
- Semrush Advertising Research and PPC Keyword Tool give competitor paid keywords, CPC and Competitive Density, and volume "down to city level" · (b) source 46 and Semrush feature pages (c). Auction Insights only shows who overlaps with you on your own keywords, not their keywords.

### 7. Low search volume, volume floors and quarantine

- A keyword with "very little to no search history" worldwide over 12 months goes inactive and re-activates automatically within about a week if traffic returns; no harm in keeping it · (a) source 7. Observed threshold roughly 10 to 20 searches a month · (c) source 53. Fix is to broaden the match type or consolidate variants · (b) source 52.
- Per-keyword floor of about 50 a month is a practitioner convention · (b) source 29. Under phrase match, thin sub-terms are matched by their parent anyway, so the floor is for what you type as a keyword, not for what you can capture.
- A "no top-of-page bid and no competition" reading means Google has too little advertiser data, not that the term is worthless · inference from (a) sources 1, 2. Judge those terms by their parent, as the existing file already says.

### 8. Budget to clicks to leads

- Clicks = budget ÷ expected CPC, using the midpoint of top-of-page low and high as the CPC guess and rounding up for a new account · (c) source 51 plus (a) source 10.
- Leads = clicks × the subcategory conversion rate from source 21 as a prior, then replace with the account's own rate.
- Smart Bidding wants at least 30 conversions a month (50 for tROAS) · (a) source 14. Consolidate: a campaign with 10 ad groups at 5 conversions each still trains on 50 · (c) source 64 and snippets. Do not split a small budget across many campaigns.
- Practitioner rule for how many ad groups a budget can test: each needs enough clicks to produce a conversion signal in a month. At 7% conversion, about 15 clicks gives one lead; a group under about 50 clicks a month cannot be read in 30 days · derived, (c). The existing file's "two ad groups on $2,000 at $15 a click" example is consistent.
- Home services CPC increased for 75% of businesses year on year and CTR for 88% · (a) source 21. Budget maths done last year is stale.

### 9. Negatives

- Negatives do not match close variants; Google says to add plurals and synonyms yourself · (a) source 6. Karooya reports Google introduced automatic misspelling coverage for negatives in 2025 · (b) source 42. Google's help page as read today still says close variants are not matched. Where they conflict, follow (a): keep adding plurals and synonyms; misspelling negatives may be redundant now but cost nothing.
- Negative match types: broad negative blocks when all words are present in any order; phrase negative needs the words in order; exact negative needs the whole query · (a) source 6.
- Limits: 1,000 account-level negatives applies to Display and Video · (a) source 6. Search negative keyword lists hold up to 5,000 terms each, with 20 lists per account and 10,000 negatives per campaign · (b) from prior Google documentation, not re-read today. PMax rose from 100 to 10,000 per campaign in March 2025 · (b) source 42. The existing file's "1,000 per shared list" is wrong for Search.
- Over-blocking is the modern failure mode: "people are just overusing negative keywords" · (b) source 26. Optmyzr's 2024 PMax study found account-level exclusions made almost no difference to CPA ($21.45 vs $18.55) or conversion rate (0.24 points) · (a) source 26. The "20 to 30% of wasted spend saved" figure in the existing file has no traceable study behind it; the best available number is WordStream's "up to three times higher conversion rate" for accounts using negatives · (a) source 22.
- Account-level shared lists for jobs, free (B2B), definition, training, template; campaign-level exclusions tied to intent boundaries; weekly search-term review early · (b) source 27.
- Search terms report hides queries below a privacy threshold since 1 Sep 2020 · (a) source 9. Hidden share at the time: 28% of spend and 20% of clicks (b) source 24; hidden clicks on Search went from 1.2% to 20.9% and hidden cost to 29.9% (b) source 25. Hidden queries had higher CPCs. So negatives found in the report are only part of the waste; n-grams on what is visible are the rest.
- Ad-group exact negatives of your other keywords keep a broad keyword from eating them · (b) source 19.

### 11. Intent classification

- SEL buckets (b) source 29: brand, competitor, third-party brand, generic category, generic specific, top-of-funnel problems.
- For local trades the working split is hire, research, DIY, job seeker, parts and retail, wrong business (commercial, rental, other trade) · (b) the existing repo taxonomy, consistent with source 27's negative categories and source 58's "job searches are 10 to 15% of wasted spend in local campaigns" (c).

### 12. PMax, AI Max, LSA implications

- AI Max, launched 6 May 2025 (a) source 16: search term matching = broad match plus keywordless matching from keywords, assets and landing pages; text customization; final URL expansion; brand inclusions and exclusions; ad-group "location of interest" control; a new "AI Max" match type value in the search terms report · (a) source 8. Google's claim: 14% more conversions at similar CPA, 27% for accounts mostly on exact and phrase · (a) as a claim, source 16.
- Independent findings: AI Max treats existing exact and phrase keywords as also broad, blending match-type data so you can no longer read match type performance · (b) source 32. It often claims credit for queries existing keywords already earned; brand terms trigger non-brand queries and vice versa · (b) sources 31, 32. Median CPA rose 16% and invalid traffic rose from 3.7% to 5% across independent tests; one case had 69% of impressions on competitor brand terms · (b) source 40. Some "exact" conversions were in fact AI Overview and AI Mode placements only reachable with AI Max on · (b) source 40.
- Broad match is the only match type eligible for AI Overview and AI Mode placements · (b) source 41. Those placements trigger on 3 to 7% of shopping and local queries versus 74% of research queries · (b) source 40. For a local trade this is not yet a reason to go broad.
- Keyword lists are "less central" but exact keywords "remain powerful across most scenarios"; start exact, layer phrase, test AI Max only on campaigns with history · (b) source 37.
- LSA moves into Google Ads as Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals, US home services from August 2026, wider late 2026, non-US 2027; it stays keywordless and targets by service category and area · (a) source 15. Search campaigns still win on control and lead quality; LSA on setup cost; run both and compare CPL through call tracking · (b) source 35.
- Smart Bidding Exploration (27% more unique converting users claimed), demand-led pacing and campaign total budgets arrived at GML 2026; journey-aware bidding in beta learns from phone calls and form fills · (a) source 17.

## Myths, with dates

- **"Exact match means the exact words."** False since 2014 for plurals and misspellings, since 2017 for function words and reordering, since Sep 2018 for same-meaning variants · (a) source 13, (b) source 39.
- **"Phrase match needs the words in order."** False since Jul 2021 when phrase absorbed modified broad · (b) source 43.
- **"Broad match plus Smart Bidding is the best default for everyone."** Google says broad is the default match type (a) source 11 and claims 25% more conversions (a) source 4. The three large independent studies from 2024 and 2025 have exact winning on CPA and conversion rate, with broad closing the gap · (a) sources 18, 19, 20. For a lead-gen account without 30 conversions a month, phrase then exact is the evidence-backed default.
- **"Negatives block misspellings and plurals too."** Google's help page still says no · (a) source 6. Misspelling coverage was reported in 2025 · (b) source 42. Plurals and synonyms are still on you.
- **"$1 of spend unlocks exact Keyword Planner numbers."** No documented threshold exists · (b) source 50. Accounts with live campaigns still report ranges at times · (c) source 70.
- **"Shared negative lists cap at 1,000."** That is the Display and Video account cap · (a) source 6. Search lists take 5,000 each · (b).
- **"SKAGs give control."** Close variants ended that; Kirk Williams called match-type segmentation dead in May 2022 · (b) source 38.
- **"Presence or interest is fine because Google recommends it."** Google's uplift claim is for Travel, Real Estate and Education · (c) source 57. It is the default because it reaches more, not because it converts more for a plumber.
- **"20 to 35% of budget leaks outside the service area."** Circulates in agency blogs (sources 57, 58) without a method. Measure it from the account's own location report.
- **"Negatives cut 20 to 30% of wasted spend."** No traceable study. WordStream's "up to 3x conversion rate" (a) source 22 is the citable number; Optmyzr's PMax study found account-level exclusions barely moved CPA · (a) source 26.
- **"The Quality Score components are weighted 39/39/22."** Google publishes no weights. Seen in one snippet, not found in the article it was attributed to · (c).
- **"AI Max finds you new queries."** Often it re-labels queries your keywords already matched · (b) sources 31, 32, 40.
- **"City ad groups give you geographic control."** You cannot geo-target an ad group, and AI Max routed "near me" queries to the wrong ad group even with identical keywords · (b) source 32.
- **"Low search volume keywords hurt the account."** Google: no negative impact; they reactivate on their own · (a) source 7.

## Rules a keyword-research command should enforce

Market and setup

1. Ask the country and language before any pull and print them on screen; Keyword Planner volume and bids are per location, language and network · (a).
2. Pull the whole service area as one `geo_target_constants` set; never sum per-city pulls, they are rounded and do not add · (a).
3. Pull `GOOGLE_SEARCH` only unless the account will run partners; partner volume inflates the number · (a).
4. Say whether the account shows exact volumes or ranges, and mark every ranged number ESTIMATE; do not promise a spend figure that unlocks exact numbers · (b).

Keywords and the city

5. Keywords are bare service terms; the city is never in the keyword text · (a) on mechanism, Jono's ruling stands.
6. Keep "near me", "nearby", "local", "in my area" as keywords; they fire on device location · (a).
7. Campaign location setting is Presence, never the default Presence or interest · (a) for what the settings do, (c) for the size of the leak. Quantify the leak from the account's own location report.
8. Cities are checked only to set the geo boundary, rank later bid adjustments, and flag demand outside the area · Jono's ruling, consistent with (a) source 14 (location is a bid signal) and (b) source 30 (localized forecasting).
9. A city earns its own ad group only by graduating on live data · Jono's ruling, (b).
10. Do not add misspelled keywords; exact and phrase already match misspellings · (a). Add plurals and synonyms of NEGATIVES, since negatives do not match variants · (a).

Match types

11. Default for a new lead-gen account with under 30 conversions a month is phrase, with proven queries promoted to exact · (a) on the studies, (b) on the sequencing.
12. Broad only with a conversion-based Smart Bidding strategy, 30+ conversions a month (50 for tROAS), shared negatives live, brand exclusions set, and someone reading search terms weekly · (a) sources 4, 14; (b) sources 27, 34.
13. After any bid strategy change, re-check match types for an unexpected broad toggle · (b).
14. Never segment by match type across ad groups or campaigns; one theme holds all its match types · (b).
15. When broad is added, put the ad group's exact keywords in as exact negatives on the broad group, or keep broad in the same group so the identical-query rule routes it · (a) source 12, (b) source 19.

Ad groups

16. 5 to 15 close synonyms per ad group sharing one ad and one page; over 15 means two themes · (b).
17. Separate emergency, planned install, and price-shopping intents into different groups even inside one trade · (b), consistent with Quality Score relevance (a).
18. Do not pre-build ad groups for terms under roughly 50 searches a month in the service area; let phrase on the parent catch them and quarantine the term with its parent named · (b).

Qualifying and volume

19. Keep a term when Keyword Planner shows advertiser competition and a top-of-page bid; that is the hire-intent signal · inference from (a).
20. A term with no bid and no competition is unpriced, not dead; judge it by its parent · inference from (a).
21. Use `monthly_search_volumes` to flag seasonality before deciding a term is small · (a).
22. Keep every cut in a quarantine section with the reason; a wrong cut is invisible forever · Jono's ruling.

Money

23. CPC guess = midpoint of top-of-page low and high, rounded up for a new account; label it as Google's 20th to 80th percentile of the last 30 days · (a) on the definition, (c) on the midpoint.
24. Clicks = budget ÷ CPC guess; leads = clicks × the LocaliQ subcategory conversion rate until the account has its own · (a) for the benchmark, derived for the maths.
25. An ad group needs roughly 50 clicks a month to be read; count how many groups the budget can honestly test and say it before launch · derived, (c).
26. Say when LSA cost per lead in the vertical undercuts the Search estimate, and note LSA is moving into Google Ads as PMax pay-per-lead from Aug 2026 · (a), (b).
27. Refresh benchmarks every year; home services CPC rose for 75% of businesses in the last measured year · (a).

Negatives

28. Account-level shared lists for job seekers, DIY, education, media and support terms go in before launch; hold price, quote, free and cheap until 30 to 60 days of search terms exist · (b) sources 26, 27, existing file.
29. Remove every serviced city from any geo negative list and verify · Jono's ruling.
30. Weekly search-term review in the first 60 days, then n-grams, because 20 to 30% of clicks are hidden from the report · (b) sources 24, 25.
31. Treat negative lists as 5,000 per list and 20 lists per account for Search; do not cite 1,000 · (b).

Competitors and sources

32. Use Keyword Planner URL and site seeds on the top three local competitors before paying for Semrush · (a) on the feature.
33. Auction Insights names competitors; Semrush Advertising Research lists their keywords; do not confuse the two · (b).
34. Autocomplete and People Also Ask are for phrasing and symptom discovery only, never for volume · (c).

AI Max and PMax

35. Do not switch AI Max on at launch; it blends match-type reporting and can re-label existing conversions. Test it only on a campaign with history, and read the "AI Max" match type in the search terms report separately · (b) sources 31, 32, 37, 40.
