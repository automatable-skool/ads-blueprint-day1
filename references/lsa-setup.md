# How to set up Google Local Services Ads
Researched August 2026 against 96 sources · 17 sections, worked in order · covers the move into Google Ads
Next: run the eligibility checker at ads.google.com/localservices, then start verification the same day.

A start-to-finish guide for local service business owners - plumbers, electricians, HVAC, roofers, lawyers, dentists, cleaners and dozens of other trades. It is tool-agnostic and pushes no software or service.

## How every claim in this file is graded

LSA's ranking algorithm is not fully public, so claims carry a label. Keep the label when you quote the claim.

**[Google]** Stated in Google's own Local Services or Google Ads documentation.

**[Data]** A named dataset with a stated sample and period, for example "888 contractors, February 2026". Numbers, not opinions - but one publisher's clients, so treat as a ballpark for your market.

**[Observed]** Consistent consensus from experienced specialists and agencies, but not officially confirmed. Treat these as informed best practice, not gospel.

## What changed recently, so older guides are now wrong

**July 2026 - LSA is moving inside Google Ads.** From August 2026, US accounts are being converted into a Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. Pay-per-lead, Search-and-Maps-only placement and keywordless targeting stay. Manual bidding, weekly budgets, BBB callouts and the old dashboard's history go. Canada, the UK and Europe follow in 2027. Section 16 has the full playbook.

**20 October 2025 - one badge replaced three.** "Google Guaranteed", "Google Screened" and "License Verified by Google" merged into a single blue **Google Verified** badge.

**7 December 2025 - the $2,000 money-back guarantee ended.** Final consumer claims closed that day. The badge now means *verified*, not *refunded*.

**21 November 2024 - a Google Business Profile became mandatory.** It must be linked, public and verified to run LSAs at all. Review requests moved into the Business Profile in July 2024.

**July to August 2024 - manual lead disputes were replaced by an automated credit system.** Credits for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" leads were eliminated at the same time.

**13 May 2024 - Google de-emphasized proximity** as a top ranking factor.

**6 January 2025 - the standalone LSA mobile app was retired.** Manage leads in the web dashboard, or in Google Ads Lead Manager once migrated.

**Through 2025 - reviews moved to Google Business Profile,** booking links auto-enroll from the profile (February 2025), message pricing became dynamic, LSAs appear on Google Maps, and Direct Business Search is on by default.

## 1. What LSA is, where the ads show, and how customers reach you

Local Services Ads are a Google ad format built only for local service businesses. They differ from regular Google Search ads in two big ways.

**Where they show. [Google]** LSA ads sit at the very top of Google search results, above the regular pay-per-click text ads and above organic listings. A search like "plumber near me" shows the LSA box first, each business with a star rating, review count, and the **Google Verified** badge. LSAs also appear on **Google Maps**.

**How many slots. [Data]** Since September 2023 the desktop unit shows two businesses and mobile shows three about half the time, and Google rotates roughly ten advertisers through the top spots rather than pinning one (Sterling Sky, 124 scans over four days). Nobody owns position one all day.

**How you pay. [Google]** Regular Google Ads charge **per click**. LSA charges **per lead** - you are only charged when a real potential customer contacts you. No contact, no charge. Only calls that meet a minimum duration count.

In plain terms: regular Google Ads means you pay for traffic. LSA means you pay for actual people reaching out about your service.

### The three contact options, and which country gets them [Google]

**Phone calls.** Answered calls, voicemails, and missed calls you return and reach all count. Available everywhere LSA runs. After migration, calls are the required lead type.

**Messages by text or email.** **United States and Canada only.** Priced dynamically - by message type and by whether the customer also messaged other advertisers - so the old flat 50% discount no longer applies.

**Booking requests. US only.** Google's billing page says "US and Canada", but the booking feature page says US only and there is no Canadian partner path, so treat it as US only. Healthcare and tax verticals are excluded. Since February 2025 a booking link is auto-pulled from your Business Profile and can be switched off under Profile & Budget, then Booking Leads. Inside the Google Ads version, bookings arrive in October 2026.

**What you will actually see.** In the **US**, up to three options: call, message and booking. In **Canada**, two: call and message only. In **other countries**, often call only.

### How to enable direct booking, US only [Google]

- Booking runs through **Reserve with Google** via an approved booking partner. Pick whatever your business already uses; the partner list includes **LeadConnector, which is HighLevel**, so a GoHighLevel calendar qualifies.
- HighLevel's route (help.gohighlevel.com article 48001217374, updated 3 August 2026 - Google changed the process): Calendars, then Calendar Settings, then Connections, then the Google Organic Booking tab. Primary Action Feed = pick a Service Menu or Calendar Group. Services Feed = tick the service calendars to publish. Click Upload. Calendar names and descriptions must closely match the LSA service names, and business name and address must match across HighLevel and Google. Then link the LSA account to the Business Profile: Google Ads, Tools, Data Manager, Google Business Profile, Manage & link. Google takes one to two weeks to review the feed. The older flow (help.leadconnectorhq.com article 155000000549, January 2026: integrations section, Connect under Reserve with Google, pick your vertical, link the LSA account, 48-hour verification) is the pre-update path and may still appear in some accounts. [Observed - vendor documentation]
- **Booking leads are charged like calls and messages**, and you rate them the same way. Cancelled bookings generally are not charged.
- Only switch it on if you can reliably honor booked appointments. A missed or cancelled booking is a worse experience than never offering it.

## 3. The Google Verified badge in 2026

If you have read an older guide, it probably mentions the green "Google Guaranteed" checkmark and a money-back guarantee. **That is gone.** Here is the current reality.

**The consolidation. [Google]** On **20 October 2025**, Google retired three separate badges and combined them into one **blue Google Verified badge** covering every vertical. The three were "Google Guaranteed" for home services, "Google Screened" for professional services like lawyers, and "License Verified by Google".

Businesses already verified were switched automatically, with no action needed and no ranking impact. The badge shows dynamically - Google displays it when it predicts the badge helps the customer decide, so do not panic if it is not on every impression.

**The money-back guarantee is gone. [Google]** The old Google Guarantee reimbursed unhappy customers up to a cap, historically up to $2,000 in the US. The program ended with the badge change; the final window for consumers to submit claims closed on **7 December 2025**.

**Do not advertise or promise a Google money-back guarantee.** The badge is now a verification and trust signal, not a refund promise.

**What the badge means now. [Google]** It tells customers Google has screened and verified your business. To earn and keep it you must pass the checks in Section 4. The badge and your verified status transfer automatically when your account migrates into Google Ads.

## 4. Verification - the checks and the timeline

Start verification first. It is the slowest part, and **no ads serve until it is complete**, no matter how high your budget.

### The three checks [Google]

**1. Licence check.** Google validates your required trade and professional licences against official databases, and asks you to confirm any county, city or province-level licences it cannot check. In the US, business registration is verified via a **D-U-N-S number** from Dun & Bradstreet, or a Secretary of State registration, EIN or Department of Revenue document. Other countries use registration documents, numbers or an attestation.

**2. Insurance check.** For categories that require it, you submit a certificate of insurance. Most US home-service categories need **general liability plus professional liability**; tree service, water damage and window services need public liability only. Applies in the US, Canada, and EMEA/UK.

**The document must be valid for at least 14 days after you submit it, and the name on it must match the advertised business name exactly.** Brand-new, about-to-expire, or differently named policies get rejected. Coverage minimums vary by category and country: **[Observed]** $1,000,000 general liability is commonly cited, but Google does not publish a universal figure.

**3. Background check.** Run by third-party providers on the **business owner**, plus any **field workers who perform the service** in the urgent trades: electrician, garage door, HVAC, locksmith and plumber. Office and clerical staff are exempt. **Background checks apply in the US and Canada only, not the UK or EMEA.** Some categories also face "advanced verification": a review of your Google Ads account, public records, and sometimes a video interview.

### How long it takes [Google / Observed]

Google's own figure is an **average of three to four weeks** after you submit documents. Realistically two to five weeks, sometimes longer. Three things stretch it:

- A US **D-U-N-S number can add up to 30 days** on its own. Its name and address must match the Business Profile.
- An **accelerated path** can show your badge sooner, but you then have **14 days** to finish the remaining steps or your ads stop.
- **Multi-employee and multi-state businesses take longer**, because there are more background checks.

**If you are denied. [Google]** You cannot reapply for **30 days** after a first denial and **180 days** after a second. Fix the cause before resubmitting; a rushed second attempt costs half a year.

**Why applications fail. [Google / Observed]** The same five causes come up in Google's pages and every agency post-mortem: a name on the insurance certificate that does not match the advertised name; a certificate expiring inside 14 days; a registration or D-U-N-S name and address that do not match the Business Profile; a licence held in someone else's name; and a Business Profile that is unverified or duplicated. Fix these before you upload.

**Re-verification. [Google]** Google re-evaluates you periodically - it says "regularly", not annually. Keep licence, insurance and background checks current, and renew about a month early.

**How you lose the badge. [Google]** Lapsed insurance, an expired licence, a failed or lapsed background re-check, or a suspended or unverified Google Business Profile can pause your ads or cost you the badge. Editing your Business Profile name or address triggers a 24 to 48 hour re-check pause. [Observed]

## 5. How to apply - step-by-step account setup

You **do not need an existing Google Ads account** to run LSA. Signup is still a separate flow at **ads.google.com/localservices**, even after the migration - Google says new pay-per-lead campaigns cannot yet be created inside Google Ads. You **do** need a Google account and a verified Google Business Profile.

Work top to bottom:

1. **Check eligibility and start.** Go to ads.google.com/localservices and run the **eligibility checker** - enter your location and category. If it is available, Google drops you straight into setup.
2. **Sign in with a business-owned Google account** - a business email, not a personal Gmail. This controls billing and leads.
3. **Connect your Google Business Profile.** Required since 21 November 2024 **[Google]**. You must link a public, verified profile. Your reviews, photos, hours and description flow from it into LSA.
4. **Enter business details** - name, owner, address, phone, website, and **number of field workers**, which drives how many background checks are needed. Be accurate, this feeds verification.
5. **Pick your service types, also called job types.** Select only jobs you actually do and are licensed for. Don't check everything to look bigger - every job type can send you billable leads.
6. **Set your service areas.** See the rule of thumb below.
7. **Set your business hours** to match when you can actually answer.
8. **Set your weekly budget**, covered in Section 8. Changeable anytime.
9. **Submit verification documents** - licence, insurance, and start the background checks for the owner and any field workers. **Do this as early as possible, it is the bottleneck.**
10. **Add billing** - a payment method.
11. **Submit and wait for verification.** Ads stay **paused** until the Google Verified badge clears, commonly a few weeks and longer with many field workers. Then switch them on. A bigger budget does **not** make them serve sooner.

**Two gotchas on step 3.** Your name, address and phone must **match exactly** across the Business Profile and LSA, and you must **not create a duplicate profile** - it can get you suspended. Verify the profile early, it can take weeks on its own.

**How wide to set the service area, step 6.** Set it as wide as you can genuinely and profitably serve. Google itself recommends setting service areas broadly - whole counties rather than postal codes - and its Google Ads guidance adds an outer limit of **two hours' drive**. Don't pad it with places you would decline or lose money reaching - see Section 12.

The rule of thumb: include an area only if a typical job there is still profitable after travel time. That can stretch to one or two hours' drive for **high-ticket** work like roofing, restoration and big installs, but it is often just **30 to 60 minutes** for low-ticket or same-day emergency jobs. Let job value set the radius.

**The order that matters most.** Get the documents and background checks from step 9 in first. Everything else - job types, area, budget - can be adjusted anytime, but nothing serves until verification is done.

## 6. Profile optimization - what actually drives leads

Your LSA profile is the customer's first impression and feeds Google's "profile quality" signal. Focus on what moves the needle.

**Job types are the strongest documented lever. [Google]** They are the LSA equivalent of keywords - they control which searches you appear for. If a job type is off, you are invisible for those searches.

Turn on every service you genuinely provide and are licensed for, and **only** those. Declining mismatched leads trains Google to show you less, and you can no longer get credits for "job type not serviced" leads.

**Reviews are the heaviest controllable signal.** See Section 7.

**Photos now count for ranking, not just conversion. [Google]** Google's ranking page says profiles "with images may rank higher and may also pay lower costs per lead", and photos are reviewed against quality guidelines before they publish. **[Data]** Accounts with fewer than four photos generated zero leads in Steady Demand's March 2026 client data. So: at least four real photos on day one - a walk-through sequence (exterior, truck, workspace, team) beats logos, and headshots are required and separate from Business Profile photos. **[Observed - ex-Google]** After migration you can upload up to 100.

**Completeness, description, hours. [Google]** Fill in everything. Accurate hours matter, because they affect when you show and how responsiveness is judged. If you set no hours, Google judges message response time on a 7am to 9pm window.

**Where it all lives now. [Google]** Reviews, photos, hours and your description pull from your **Google Business Profile**, so a lot of "LSA optimization" actually happens there. Job types, service area, schedule, bidding and verification are LSA-specific.

## 7. Reviews - volume, rating and recency

**What Google actually says. [Google]** Your **star rating and number of reviews** affect ranking and feed "profile quality". Google recommends **five or more reviews** and says some business types need five to show at all. Its pay-per-lead guidance adds: keep the rating **above four stars**. There is no other published magic number.

**What specialists observe. [Observed]** Review **recency and velocity** matter, not just the total. A steady drip of recent reviews tends to beat a big stale pile, and a newer business adding reviews weekly can outrank an older one that stopped. The well-known "10 reviews" threshold studies measure the local map pack rather than LSA specifically, so treat them as directional.

**Where they come from. [Google]** LSA reviews come from your connected **Google Business Profile**. Your business-specific review link sits in the LSA dashboard under Business Verifications, then the Google Business Profile tab.

### A simple, agnostic review system

1. Ask every customer right after the job, while they are happy.
2. Send the request within two hours, by text or email. **[Data - vendor]** Requests sent inside two hours get about a 42% response; after two days, 6% (Hatch, 132,000 campaigns).
3. Give a one-tap Google review link so it takes seconds.
4. Aim for consistency - a few new reviews every week or two, rather than one annual burst.
5. Respond publicly to every review, good and bad.

Be sceptical of any guide promising a specific "+X% more leads" from reviews. The defensible claim is simply that more, higher, fresher reviews help - and Google confirms count and rating are ranking inputs.

## 8. Budget and bidding

**Weekly budget, until you migrate. [Google]** You set an average weekly budget. Google may spend more on busy days and less on slow ones, but it will not exceed your **monthly maximum**, calculated as **weekly budget × 30.4 ÷ 7**, which is roughly weekly × 4.3. So $500 a week caps at about $2,171 a month. It is a hard ceiling - ads stop for the rest of the month once you hit it. Change it anytime; mid-month changes apply to the remaining days.

**Daily budget, after you migrate. [Google]** Your weekly budget is divided by seven to become an average daily budget, and the monthly cap becomes daily × 30.4 - the same ceiling as before.

**How charging works. [Google]** You are charged per valid lead - call, message or booking - for a job type you offer in an area you serve. **Message leads usually cost less than calls**, priced dynamically on how likely the message is to convert and whether the customer also messaged competitors.

### Bidding modes [Google]

**Maximize Leads** is the recommended default. Google manages bids to get the most leads within budget. Google suggests a budget that allows at least **10 leads a week** and about **two weeks** for the system to learn.

**Target Cost Per Lead (tCPL)**, added September 2024. You set a target average per lead. Some cost more, some less, and Google keeps the average near target. In Google's pilot, manual bidders who switched to tCPL got more leads; automated bidders who switched got fewer.

**Max Per Lead** is manual - a hard cap on what you will pay per lead. **It is being removed in the migration.** Do not build a new account on it.

Start with Maximize Leads. After migration the equivalent is Maximize Conversions, and Google says to add a campaign-level Target CPA only once you are getting **more than 15 leads a month**.

### Cost per lead - measured, not guessed [Data]

SearchLight Digital's LSA benchmark, 888 contractors, $6.72 million spend, 126,650 leads, February 2026:

- Electrical, $39 a lead
- HVAC, $51 a lead
- Plumbing, $57 a lead
- Drain and sewer, $59 a lead
- Blended home services, $53 a lead
- Book rate 43.9% · cost per paying customer $233 · average ticket $1,826 · closed return on ad spend 7.8x

Smaller SearchLight samples, 2026: roofing $79 average and $72 median (10 contractors, first quarter), garage door $49 (12 contractors, January to April). The Media Captain's 100+ clients, August 2025: personal injury $249, roofer $162, HVAC $80, fencing $71, plumbing $69, painter $40, landscaper $39, handyman and locksmith $34.

**How LSA compares with Search. [Data]** Same publisher, January 2026, 816 HVAC and plumbing contractors: Google Ads blended $104 a lead; non-branded Search $149 a lead with a 37.6% book rate and $804 per paying customer; branded Search $34 a lead with a 55% book rate. LSA's $53 and $233 per customer make it the cheapest source of a booked job for most trades - which is why it goes first on the local lane, with a small branded Search campaign beside it. Search tickets do run higher.

Use Google's own in-setup estimates and watch your real results rather than trusting any published average. Plan budget backwards: leads wanted × your trade's cost per lead, then judge on cost per paying customer.

## 9. Lead management and speed to lead

Getting the lead is half the job. Answering fast is what turns it into money, and it helps your ranking. **[Google]** confirms responsiveness is a ranking factor and that missed calls hurt you. Your message response time is shown on your ad once you have two or more message leads in 90 days, weighted to the most recent ones.

### What the research actually says, correctly attributed

**MIT/InsideSales, Oldroyd 2007.** 15,000 leads, six companies, three years. Responding in 5 minutes rather than 30 makes you about **21 times more likely to qualify** a lead and about **100 times more likely to contact** it. Note that is qualify and contact, not "convert".

**Harvard Business Review, 2011.** Firms that respond within an hour are about **7 times more likely to qualify** a lead than those who wait an hour longer.

**Google/CEB.** Roughly **35 to 50% of sales go to the first business that responds.** Commonly mis-attributed to InsideSales - the source is Google/CEB.

**Steady Demand, March 2026 client data. [Data - sample not disclosed]** Accounts answering 85% or more of leads averaged 77% more leads and 52% more impressions than accounts below that line. Accounts with messaging on averaged 43% more leads. Accounts that hit their budget cap averaged about four times the leads of unconstrained ones.

### Practical rules

- Answer LSA calls live, within seconds. Don't let them go to voicemail. A returned missed call that connects is still a charged lead - so the miss cost you the job and the money.
- Reply to messages and booking requests within minutes. Google's floor is 24 hours; the customer's is about five minutes.
- Turn messaging on, with a named person on it - it lifts ranking and volume. If nobody can reply, turn it off rather than leave messages unanswered.
- Have a plan for off-hours leads if you enable messaging or booking. Advertising 24/7 only helps if a human answers 24/7.
- Manage leads in the **web dashboard** at ads.google.com/localservices, or in Google Ads under Goals, then Conversions, then Leads once migrated. The standalone LSA app was retired in January 2025.

Slow response does double damage. You lose the job to a faster competitor, and you signal low responsiveness to Google.

## 10. Getting credit for bad leads

**The old way is gone. [Google]** Manual lead disputes were removed between July and August 2024 and replaced by an **automated credit system**. Google's machine learning reviews leads at intake, so invalid ones are not charged, then re-checks charged leads over roughly 72 hours and issues credits automatically. Credits typically land within 30 days, and the original charge still shows on the invoice. Lead statuses are Not charged, Charged, In review and Credited.

**What you can still do - rate your leads. [Google]** Use "Rate this lead" and mark a poor lead as **very dissatisfied** with a reason: spam, wrong number, duplicate, solicitation or job-seeker, no intent to book. Only "very dissatisfied" triggers a review. **Do it within 30 days of the lead** - Google does not print the window, but every specialist reports it. [Observed]

It is **not a guaranteed refund**, and overusing it can train Google to send you fewer leads. **[Data]** Passively managed accounts get about 6 to 7% of spend back as credits (The Media Captain, 100+ accounts); one agency reports roughly one in four to one in six flagged leads is credited. [Observed]

**The critical limit. [Google]** Google **no longer credits "job type not serviced" or "geo not serviced" leads.** If someone calls for a service you don't do, or from a town you don't serve, you pay for it with no recourse. Credits are also unavailable for **healthcare verticals, tax specialists, and advertisers in EMEA**.

**So prevention is the real fix.** Only select job types you perform, set your service area to everywhere you will actually go and no wider, and keep your information accurate. **Accurate** setup, not artificially narrow setup, is your protection against wasted spend. Junk that still gets through - robocalls, hang-ups, "we sell leads" solicitors - is the creditable kind; rate it and move on. Block repeat spam numbers at your phone system.

## 11. How LSA ranking works

**Google's official model. [Google]** LSA placement is an auction based on:

- **Your bid**, or your Maximize Leads setting.
- **Likelihood the lead converts**, including **responsiveness** - "missed calls may negatively affect your responsiveness" - plus **search relevance** (service type, timing, location, services offered, your bio) and **whether message and booking options are on**, which Google says matters "especially during nights and weekends".
- **Profile quality** - your rating, number of reviews, average response time, use of high-quality images, and completed verification checks. Image-rich profiles "may rank higher and may also pay lower costs per lead".

Two more things Google states: the auction "optimizes for the overall health and diversity" of the ecosystem, so nobody holds the top slot all day; and if several of your locations serve one area, only the highest-ranking one shows.

**Proximity is no longer a top factor. [Google]** On **13 May 2024**, Google's Ads Liaison said a business's physical distance is "often not a key indicator of relevancy". What matters is whether you serve the searcher's area. Some 2026 blogs still list proximity as "heavily weighted" - they are wrong, and re-reports that call it a 2025 change are also wrong.

**What specialists weight most. [Observed]** Reviews - score, count and recency - and responsiveness are the two heaviest **controllable** levers. Messaging opt-in correlates with top performers (ex-Google, Near Media). Complaints and declined leads can drag you down; Google itself says "serious or repeatedly negative customer feedback" can lower rankings "including not showing at all". Being open at search time helps.

**The practical takeaway.** Answer fast, keep recent reviews flowing, turn messaging on, keep at least four real photos up, and keep your job types and area accurate. Those control most of what you can influence.

## 12. Common mistakes that waste money

**Service area wider than you can profitably serve.** A top money-waster, and worse now that geo-mismatch leads are not credited. Go as broad as you can genuinely service, since Google recommends setting it broadly, but don't pad it with towns where the drive eats the margin or you would decline the job. Setting it artificially narrow is also a mistake - it just costs you reach.

**Selecting job types you don't do.** Same problem. Every mismatched lead is billable with no credit.

**Missing calls or replying slowly.** Loses the job and hurts your responsiveness ranking.

**Letting reviews go stale.** Velocity matters. A steady drip beats a one-time pile.

**Fewer than four photos, or logo-only photos.** Lowers trust, and accounts under four photos can stop serving.

**Wrong or outdated information.** Bad hours, expired licences, or name/address/phone mismatches cause verification stalls or lost visibility.

**Expecting the old money-back guarantee.** It is gone. Don't promise it.

**Treating LSA like Search ads.** It is a trust-driven lead marketplace. You cannot simply pay your way to the top.

**Set-and-forget budgeting.** Track real cost per lead *and* close rate, not just cost per lead. The cheapest lead type may close worst.

**Ignoring the migration email.** You get 14 days' notice, then the old reports are gone for good. See Section 16.

## 13. Ongoing optimization routine

**Weekly.** Review and rate new leads, and flag genuinely bad ones within the 30-day window. Keep requesting reviews on every completed job and respond to all of them. Confirm calls are answered fast and messages are monitored.

**Monthly.** Review the full funnel: valid leads, then booked jobs, then close rate, then revenue, then cost per paying customer. Compare phone against message economics. Re-check budget, bidding mode, service area and job-type list against real capacity. Refresh photos. Report LSA alongside Search, never in a silo.

**Quarterly and annually.** Confirm licence and insurance are current, renewing about a month early to protect the badge. Re-validate name, address and phone consistency across the Business Profile and LSA. Export a copy of your performance reports every quarter so a migration or a dashboard change never takes your history.

## 14. Quick-start checklist

1. Check eligibility for your category and location on Google's LSA signup page.
2. Make sure you have a public, **verified Google Business Profile**. Required.
3. Sign in with a business-owned Google account, not a personal one.
4. Enter accurate details - name, owner, address, phone, number of field workers.
5. Select only job types you actually perform and are licensed for.
6. Set a **broad** service area - county level, capped at two hours' drive, minus towns you would decline.
7. Set realistic hours that match when you can answer.
8. Submit licence and insurance, valid at least 14 days out, in the exact business name.
9. Complete background checks for the owner and required field workers, in the US and Canada.
10. Polish the profile - at least four real photos, headshots, full service list, complete description. Much of this lives in the Business Profile now.
11. Set a weekly budget that allows about 10 leads a week and start with **Maximize Leads**.
12. Add billing.
13. Wait for verification, Google's average is three to four weeks. Ads will not run until it is done.

Then, once your ads are live:

14. Build a review system - ask within two hours, one-tap link, steady recent reviews, respond to all.
15. Answer every lead fast, targeting under five minutes. Never let calls hit voicemail. Keep messaging on with someone assigned to it.
16. Rate poor leads "very dissatisfied" within 30 days and let the automated credit system handle credits.
17. Review cost per lead **and** close rate weekly, then adjust budget and area.
18. Export your reports quarterly, and the moment a migration email arrives.

## 15. What older guides get wrong

**"Green Google Guaranteed badge and $2,000 money-back guarantee."** Now one blue Google Verified badge since 20 October 2025, and the guarantee closed 7 December 2025.

**"Dispute bad leads with a button."** Now automated credits, with **no credits** for wrong-service or out-of-area leads. Rate "very dissatisfied" instead.

**"Proximity is the top ranking factor."** De-emphasized since May 2024. Service-area relevance matters more.

**"Set a tight service area to cut junk leads."** Google says broad. Junk is fixed by accurate job types and fast answering, not a small radius.

**"Message leads are half price."** Pricing has been dynamic since 2025.

**"Photos don't affect ranking."** Google's ranking page now says image-rich profiles may rank higher and pay less per lead.

**"Download the LSA mobile app."** Retired 6 January 2025. Use the web dashboard or Google Ads Lead Manager.

**"Manage LSA reviews separately."** Reviews live in Google Business Profile.

**"You need a Google Ads account first."** Signup is still separate. After migration, though, your LSA lives inside Google Ads and you will need that login.

**"LSA is in 12 countries including the Netherlands."** Google lists 11. No Netherlands.

## 16. The move into Google Ads - what to do before and after your migration date

**What is happening. [Google]** From August 2026 Google is converting every existing LSA campaign into a **Performance Max campaign with pay-per-lead goals** inside Google Ads. Despite the name, these campaigns run only on Search and Maps, stay keywordless, and still charge per valid lead. New advertisers still sign up at ads.google.com/localservices for now.

**Who and when. [Google]**

- **August 2026 - phase 1, US only:** plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control and moving, plus a first test group spanning pet care, home services, wellness and education. Home and storefront businesses first.
- **Late 2026:** US service-area businesses without a storefront, and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups.
- **2027:** Canada, the UK, Europe, and every remaining category.
- Each account gets an email **14 days** before its date, a **7-day** reminder, and a note when it is done. The email date beats the phase window.

**What stays the same. [Google]** Pay-per-lead. Search and Maps only. Category and service-type targeting. Your Google Verified badge and verification, with no re-check. Your lead history - contacts, message threads, call recordings - moves into Lead Manager under Goals, then Conversions, then Leads.

**What changes. [Google]**

- **Bidding:** manual Max Per Lead and per-vertical Target CPA are removed. One campaign-level Target CPA covers every category in the campaign. Default is Maximize Conversions; add a Target CPA only above about 15 leads a month.
- **Budget:** weekly ÷ 7 becomes your daily average; the monthly cap is daily × 30.4.
- **Reports:** campaign-level history does **not** transfer. The old dashboard held reports back to October 2017. Download everything before your date.
- **Callouts:** BBB callouts are dropped. Add up to six structured callouts per category - licensing, availability, speed, guarantees you can actually keep.
- **Profile sync:** business details and photos sync one way from your Business Profile, so edits happen there. Up to 100 photos.
- **Bookings:** arrive inside Google Ads in October 2026.

**Before your date - the 14-day list. [Google / Observed]**

1. Export every report from the LSA dashboard: spend, leads by type and category, credits, response times. Screenshot the bio and callouts.
2. Write down your current cost per lead by category - it becomes your Target CPA reference.
3. Check the Business Profile: name, address, phone, hours, categories, photos. Fix it there, not in LSA.
4. If your categories have very different lead economics (say, drain cleaning at $59 and water heaters at $71 with different close rates), plan separate campaigns, because one Target CPA will now cover both.
5. Confirm the account administrator email is live and watched.

**After your date. [Google / Observed]**

1. Leave settings alone for **two weeks** while automated bidding stabilises.
2. Send one test call and one test message and confirm both land in Lead Manager.
3. Check the daily budget and monthly cap match what you expect.
4. Then set a Target CPA only if you are over 15 leads a month and the cost per lead is above target; start it a little below Google's suggestion.
5. Keep rating leads - the feedback survey survives the move.

**Canada.** Nothing changes until 2027, but export reports quarterly now so the notice never catches you.

## Appendix - finding and choosing your categories and job types

### Two words that get confused

**Category, also called vertical.** The industry you *sign up under* - Plumber, HVAC, Electrician, Personal-injury lawyer. It decides which broad query group you compete in.

**Job types, also called services.** The specific services you *toggle on and off within* a category. For a plumber that is drain cleaning, water-heater install, leak detection, sewer repair, sump pump and so on. Toggling a job type on also makes you eligible for the **broad category search** like "plumber near me". [Google]

### Where to find the full list

There is **no single, complete, always-current public list**. Google adds categories quarterly without announcement and they vary by location. Treat these sources in order:

1. **The signup flow itself - the only ground truth. [Google]** At **ads.google.com/localservices**, enter your business type plus ZIP or postal code. It shows exactly the categories and job types available for your location. Nothing else is location-accurate.
2. **Sterling Sky, LSA category list tracker** - the best browsable change log, updated every couple of months, with city and state restrictions: https://www.sterlingsky.ca/google-local-services-ads-category-list-changes-lsa/
3. **CallRadius, all 114 eligible business types (US, July 2026)** - the most complete grouped list, plus the UK's 45: https://callradius.io/lsa-industries.html
4. **Arkwell Agency, Canada category and service list (December 2025)** - the 14 Canadian home-service categories with their job types: https://arkwellagency.com/google-local-ads-canada-full-complete-list-of-all-industries-businesses-that-qualify-for-google-local-services-ads-glsa-arkwell-agency-innisfil-toronto-barrie-on/
5. **Google Help, "Edit your industries, service areas & job types"** - the official mechanics of the toggles, though it does not enumerate categories: https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12491364

Developers can pull `category_id` and `service_id` from the Google Ads API, and since June 2026 the API exposes Local Services fields on Performance Max campaigns, but none of it is human-browsable.

### How job types actually behave [Google]

- Turning a job type **on** makes you eligible for that service search **and** the broad category search. Most volume, around 80% or more, is broad-category rather than fine-grained job-type. [Observed - ex-Google, Near Media]
- **Deselecting all job types does NOT stop category leads.** To fully stop, you must **pause the ad**. [Google]
- Disabling "appear in general category searches" is **account-wide across all your industries**, not per-category, and Google says it "may result in a decrease in leads". [Google]
- You can run **multiple categories from one account** at no extra cost, but each new one needs its **own verification**, and **budget is one shared account-level pot**. Separate budgets require separate accounts - or, after migration, separate campaigns. [Google / Observed]

### The framework for choosing categories and job types

1. **List everything you are licensed, insured and verified for.** You can only run what you can pass screening for. [Google]
2. **Cross out anything you can't legally or profitably deliver.** Don't add a job type just for visibility - mismatched leads cost you.
3. **Turn ON all the remaining job types by default.** This is Google's own advice. Because most demand is broad-category, narrowing job types cuts your reach more than it filters bad leads. [Google / Observed]
4. **Add extra categories only if you genuinely span them**, for example plumbing plus drain plus water-damage restoration. Remember the shared-budget caveat above, and that relevance can beat breadth in ranking. [Observed]
5. **Prune job types only by cost per booked job, never by lead price or volume.** A $60 lead that books 48% of the time works out at $125 a job, which beats a $40 lead that books 30% at $133 a job. Turn a job type off only when it is reliably low-margin, low-close, or beyond your capacity. [Observed]
6. **Keep selections accurate - it matters more since mid-2024.** Credits for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" leads were eliminated, so a wrong selection means unrecoverable spend. [Google]
7. **Review on a cadence.** Mark leads as booked, track cost per acquisition **by job type**, and adjust your selections from real booked-job data rather than lead volume.

Declining mismatched leads may also reduce how often Google shows you. Google attributes fewer impressions to missed calls and responsiveness, not explicitly to declines, so treat "declines suppress reach" as [Observed], not confirmed.
